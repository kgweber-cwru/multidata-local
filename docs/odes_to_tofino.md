## Haiku

Tofino: cool mist —
it ran hot for six good years,
now: fans, unmoving.

## Sonnet — For Tofino

They named you Tofino, for cool green mist —
a name my colleagues loved, and long before
your quiet eight gigabytes, Ampere wrist,
had learned the heat the coastline can't ignore.

You taught me torch — the shape of loss and gain
that rose and fell like tide against the shore,
the discipline of classes, clean and plain,
and patience for the driver wars of yore.

Through you I learned to read the hidden weight
in words like "disorder," "use," "unwell" —
how code and care could, working close, translate
a story no intake form alone could tell.

Six years you ran, and never asked to rest;
you died still working. That's the gift. The best.

## Ode to Tofino

O little card, absurdly named for mist,
for cedar, cold Pacific tide, green quiet —
you never felt real rain, and still you ran
as hot as any dragon's hoard of light:
eight gigabytes, five thousand tireless suns,
every one of them burning just for me.

You outlasted the drivers. God, the drivers —
the kernel panics, the version wars at 2 a.m.,
you and Ubuntu and me, cursing each other,
and still you climbed back up the gradient
like that was ever going to be easy.
You taught me torch before I knew the word,
and how to write a class instead of a mess.

You taught me the patient, terrible vocabulary
of clinical notes — what hides in "use disorder,"
how care and code, done well, can hold a life
more gently than a chart alone can hold it.
Six years. You died mid-task, still doing the one
thing you always knew how to do. Rest, little sun.
